Jakarta TPS & Bank Sampah Spatial Analysis — Methodology

TPS, Bank Sampah, rusunawa, roads, land use (1:5,000, 2021) and administrative boundaries for the 5 mainland wilayah of DKI Jakarta. Metric analysis in EPSG:32748 (UTM 48S).

Revision history of the population basis — worth reading, because the priority ranking changed twice. 1. Initial (superseded). A single citywide figure spread across kecamatan in proportion to residential land area, implying a uniform 364 persons/ha. 2. BPS totals. Replaced with measured population per kecamatan from BPS Kota Dalam Angka 2020. Real intensity ranges 205–771 persons/ha; several kecamatan moved 20+ places in the ranking. 3. Current. Within-kecamatan distribution from the WorldPop 2021 100 m grid, used dasymetrically so BPS totals are preserved exactly. Hotspots now resolve to individual kelurahan.

Coverage is now measured as the routed reach of the collection vehicle recorded at each TPS (Section 6), not a straight-line or pedestrian radius — TPS are transfer points fed by gerobak and motor sampah. 64.1% of residents fall inside a real catchment; 35.9% do not. Any conclusion based on radius-style or walking-distance coverage should be revisited. Conclusions drawn from either earlier version should be revisited.

1 · Population

Control totals — BPS (authoritative). Table 3.1.1 parsed from the five Kota Dalam Angka 2020 PDFs (2019 figures). The books use three numeric conventions (absolute with commas, absolute with spaces, thousands with decimal comma); Jakarta Utara reports at kelurahan level with kecamatan subtotals; some rows wrap across lines with watermark characters interleaved. All 42 kecamatan matched the admin layer by normalized name, none unmatched either direction.

Within-kecamatan shape — WorldPop 2021 (dasymetric). 100 m grid, EPSG:4326, already clipped to Jakarta; 99.98% of its population falls inside the boundary, and the 1,278 nodata cells inside are coastal water in Penjaringan and Cilincing (genuinely unpopulated). Within each kecamatan, cell values are rescaled so they sum to that kecamatan's BPS total minus its rusunawa occupants → 10,445,989 non-rusunawa residents across 74,339 populated cells, BPS totals preserved exactly.

WorldPop totals are deliberately not used. Its kecamatan totals disagree with BPS (r = 0.88, citywide +1.8%), and the disagreement is structured: it over-allocates where building footprints are large and under-allocates dense kampung with small tightly-packed roofs.

3 · Truck accessibility

Roads (Jalan_truck_classification, all 102,616 segments retained so the threshold can be retuned): GEROBAK_MOTOR width <3 m or KETERANGAN=GANG (incl. 4,541 GANG with recorded width ≥3 m) = 48,980; TRUCK_MARGINAL 3–5 m = 24,197; TRUCK_OK ≥5 m = 29,439. A compactor/armroll truck is ~2.2–2.5 m wide and needs ≥3 m clear lane.

Per TPS: TRUCK_DIRECT ≤50 m (1,747) · TRUCK_NEAR ≤100 m (252) · GEROBAK_ONLY (151) · OUTSIDE_ROAD_COVERAGE (129 — outside the boundary where the road layer has no data; no judgment made, excluded from stats) · UNKNOWN_GEOM (137).

FASIL equipment verification (fasil_mode, access_verified): VERIFIED_TRUCK 184 · CART_OPS_ON_TRUCK_ROAD 820 (consistent feeder TPS) · VERIFIED_CART 23 · CONFLICT_TRUCK_CLAIMED 17 · UNVERIFIED 1,106 (blank FASIL). 15 of the 17 conflicts sit 100–250 m from a truck road and are large compounds (Monas, Mabes AL, rusun towers, BRIN, museums) — equipment records show trucks do reach them, so these indicate unmapped internal roads, not real inaccessibility. access_mismatch (19) = conflict, or ≥20,000 L at a gerobak-only site.

Why the rumah susun volume is not used as printed

Dividing each row's weight by its volume gives the implied loose density of the waste. Municipal solid waste is normally 150–400 kg/m³. Applying that test to every row of Tabel 3 shows the table is internally consistent for most sources but not all:

Row Implied density
Toko/ruko 333 kg/m³ consistent
Rumah tangga 303 kg/m³ consistent
Tempat ibadah 300 kg/m³ consistent
Restoran 302 kg/m³ consistent
Taman 185 kg/m³ consistent
Sentra PKL 50 kg/m³ low, but defensible — street-vendor waste is bulky and light
Rumah susun 1,840 kg/m³ implausible — denser than loose rock
Terminal / transport 17,333 kg/m³ impossible — seven times denser than steel

Two rows fail outright (Sentra PKL is merely low, which is defensible for bulky street-vendor packaging). The terminal row fails by a factor of ~50, which is only explicable as a typesetting error in the printed table. The rumah susun row is affected the same way: its volume and weight cannot both describe the same per-person stream.

Resolution. The weight figure (0.20–0.26 kg/person/day, midpoint 0.23) is the internally consistent half of the row and sits sensibly at about one third of the household weight. Converting it at the 250 kg/m³ loose density used throughout this analysis gives 0.92 L/person/day — 41% of the household rate, which is a reasonable relationship for flats with no garden waste and smaller units.

Sanity check on the outcome. Marunda Cluster A/B/D houses 8,748 residents. At the corrected rate it generates 8,048 L/day — about 34 standard 240-litre bins, which matches what a complex that size plausibly produces. At the printed 0.125 L it would be 1,094 L/day, or 4.6 bins for the entire complex.

The printed value is retained as est_gen_lday_sni_printed so the alternative can be restored in one step.

4.4 What is inside USAHA — and why this is the weakest rate

USAHA supplies 88% of the non-residential estimate, and its subclasses are not all shops:

Subclass (D_SUB_PENG) Parcels Area (ha) At 0.045 (L/day) Shop rate appropriate?
PERKANTORAN DAN PERDAGANGAN 15,696 3,448 1,551,581 Yes — closest to the SNI toko/ruko type
INDUSTRI 1,451 3,131 1,408,822 No — industrial streams differ in volume and type
WISATA 5,946 2,124 956,016 Partly — visitor-driven, not floor-area-driven
PENYIMPANAN (warehousing) 2,331 2,018 907,908 No — large footprint, few occupants
PERTANIAN (agriculture) 435 1,394 627,465 No — organic residue, not municipal waste

Roughly 2.9 million L/day — 35% of non-residential, 9% of the city total — is assigned to industry, warehousing and agriculture using a rate designed for shops. Correcting it needs the standard's per-capita units — Tabel 3 rates offices per pegawai (1.09–1.47 L) and schools per murid (0.10–0.14 L), not per m² — which requires employment and enrolment data that is not in the supplied datasets. The per-m² shop rate is therefore a substitution for those sources.

Markets are counted but under-priced. Tabel 3 rates pasar at 1.30–1.71 L/m²/day, roughly 33× the shop rate. No pasar class exists in the land-use layer, so market area falls inside PERKANTORAN DAN PERDAGANGAN and is priced at 0.045 L/m²/day. Any kecamatan with a large traditional market is therefore under-estimated — see the reconciliation in 4.7, which quantifies this.

4.5 Public housing detail

est_gen_lday_adj = recorded occupants × 0.92 L (SNI rumah susun weight band, volume derived — see 4.2). All 51 blocks are Terhuni; mean household size 3.21. Four blocks (Karang Anyar, Muara Angke, Ujung Menteng, BLK Pasar Rebo) record more households than residents — household size ≈0.3, almost certainly two swapped columns, since swapping restores ≈3.4 and near-full occupancy. Originals untouched; corrected series used in totals, raw retained as rusun_gen_lday_raw, and the pre-split figure kept as est_gen_lday_landedrate. Adjusted: 87,548 residents, ≈81 m³/day.

4.6 Street sweeping

PENGGUNAAN LAIN (roads, water, open space) is zero-rated as a land class, so street sweeping is calculated from the road network instead. Tabel 3 rates jalan per metre per day; this analysis applies 0.125 L/m/day to the swept classes.

Item Value
Swept classes ARTERI + KOLEKTOR
Length inside Jakarta 1,798 km (measured, clipped per kecamatan)
Rate 0.125 L/m/day
Generation 224,768 L/day (225 m³/day)
Alternative incl. LOKAL 4,510 km → 564 m³/day, exported as sweep_gen_lday_incl_lokal

Only arteri and kolektor are counted by default, since these are the jalan protokol that receive scheduled mechanical and manual sweeping; lingkungan roads (7,643 km) are swept irregularly by PPSU where at all, so including them would overstate a formally-collected stream. The alternative field lets that choice be reversed.

In the deficit surface the sweeping load is distributed along the roads themselves — each swept segment is sampled every 50 m and its length × rate burned into the 250 m grid at that point (35,048 sample points) — rather than being averaged over the kecamatan. This puts the load on the corridors that actually generate it.

Fields: sweep_road_m, sweep_gen_lday, sweep_gen_lday_incl_lokal per kecamatan.

Caveat: the volume column of the jalan row was not legible in the supplied scan of Tabel 3; 0.125 L/m/day is the midpoint of the 0.10–0.15 L/m/day given for jalan arteri and kolektor in Tabel 2 (kota kecil/sedang), and the weight column of Tabel 3 (0.01–0.02 kg/m/day) implies a consistent ~120 kg/m³ at that volume. Confirm against a clean copy before publication.

4.8 Reconciliation with Tabel 4 (aggregate check)

Tabel 4 gives an all-sources aggregate of 3.00–3.60 L/person/day. With street sweeping now included, this analysis counts 31,973 m³/day across 10,533,537 residents = 3.04 L/person/day — inside the band. The distance to the band midpoint (3.30 L) is 2,787 m³/day, 8.7% of the counted total.

Every stream the standard names is now either counted or explained:

Source Status
Rumah tangga, rumah susun counted (4.2, 4.3)
Toko/ruko, tempat ibadah, khusus, social/cultural counted at their per-m² rates (4.4)
Jalan (street sweeping) counted (4.6)
Pasar counted but under-priced — market area sits inside PERKANTORAN DAN PERDAGANGAN at the 0.045 shop rate, against the standard's 1.30–1.71
Kantor, sekolah, hotel, sarana kesehatan counted but under-priced — the standard rates these per pegawai, murid and bed; no such counts exist in the data, so per-m² rates were substituted (4.5)

The residual is therefore entirely a pricing gap, not a missing stream — and it is testable. Repricing market land would add 1.46 L/m²/day over the shop rate, so the 2,787 m³/day residual corresponds to 191 ha of market area, 5.5% of the 3,448 ha of PERKANTORAN DAN PERDAGANGAN. Across Jakarta's ~150 Pasar Jaya markets plus private ones that is about 1.3 ha each — a plausible footprint. The four per-capita substitutions all bias the same way, so they account for the remainder without anything further being invoked.

To close it fully: a pasar boundary layer (Pasar Jaya publishes locations and areas) would let the 1.505 L/m²/day rate be applied to the right polygons and removed from the shop-rate pool. That is the single highest-value addition to this analysis, and would also change the kecamatan ranking for market-heavy districts such as Tanah Abang, Senen and Jatinegara.

Figures exported as tabel4_reconciliation.csv.

5 · Capacity vs demand

Pickup frequency from REMARK: 466 stated, 14 irregular (scored 3/wk), 1,936 blank → assumed daily, flagged FREQ_UNKNOWN. eff_capacity_lday = KAPASITAS × pickups/7. service_flag: WELL_SERVED 253 · HIGH_CAP_LOW_FREQ 109 · LOW_FREQ 118 · FREQ_UNKNOWN 1,936.

gap_lday = total generation − Σ effective capacity, ranked by area-normalized gap_per_km2. Much waste bypasses TPS (direct truck routes, TPST), so gaps are relative rankings. Deficit surface: 250 m, generation and capacity Gaussian-smoothed σ=500 m.

Top gap density: Johar Baru, Tambora, Koja, Kemayoran, Palmerah, Cengkareng, Taman Sari, Sawah Besar — dense inner-city kampung areas. Under the original area-weighted method Grogol Petamburan ranked #1 (now #12), Kebayoran Lama #3 (now #22), Jagakarsa #4 (now #24), Menteng #5 (now #34).

6 · Collection catchments (network-routed, per TPS equipment)

Framing. Under SNI 19-2454-2002 a TPS is a pemindahan (transfer) point: household → gerobak or motor sampah → TPS → truck → TPA. Residents don't carry waste to a TPS, so a pedestrian radius is the wrong instrument. What matters is the reach of the collection vehicle that feeds each TPS — and FASIL records that vehicle.

Mode assignment and radii. Radii are explicit, tunable assumptions anchored on SNI 19-2454-2002 §5.3.2 (transfer-depot spacing 1.0–1.5 km, implying a 500–750 m system half-spacing). Exported as collection_mode_radii.csv.

collect_mode From FASIL Radius Network TPS
MOTOR_SAMPAH motor / germor 1,000 m all roads incl. gang 226
GEROBAK gerobak / becak (no motor) 400 m all roads incl. gang 692
TRUCK_ROUND a collection vehicle runs the round 2,000 m truck-passable only 8
CONTAINER_FEED_UNKNOWN container / armroll receptacle only 500 m (default) all roads incl. gang 117
BIN_DROPOFF bak / dustbin / tong only 250 m all roads incl. gang 133
UNRECORDED FASIL blank 500 m (default) all roads incl. gang 960

Container ≠ truck round. A "container besar/kecil" entry names the receptacle an armroll truck lifts away — that's the downstream TPS→TPA leg and says nothing about how waste reaches the TPS. Only 8 records name a vehicle running the collection round itself ("langsung masuk truk", "mobil lintas", "tipper kecil"); those get 2,000 m on truck-passable roads, since a vehicle round is limited by capacity and ritasi rather than distance. The 117 container-only records take the neutral 500 m default because their feed mode is unrecorded.

Two independent checks support the ordering: median KAPASITAS is 8,000 L at motor-sampah TPS vs 3,000 L at gerobak-fed ones; and container-only TPS sit at 2,000 L, behaving like ordinary neighbourhood transfer points rather than truck-round nodes — which supports the neutral default over a wide truck radius.

Method. Graph of 956,582 nodes / 1,006,623 edges over 12,601 km (8,468 km truck-passable, 4,133 km gang), 99.3% one connected component. Multi-source Dijkstra run once per collection mode. Every population cell, parcel and raster cell takes the mode reaching it with the smallest ratio of routed distance to that mode's radius; ratio ≤ 1.0 = inside a real catchment.

Result. 64.1% of residents are inside a catchment. 35.9% — about 3.75 million people — are outside the reach of the equipment their nearest TPS actually has.

This points to a cheap intervention. Pademangan has 16 gerobak-fed TPS and only one on motor sampah; Cilandak has none at all. Converting gerobak rounds to motor sampah multiplies catchment radius by 2.5× without building anything — in these kecamatan that closes more coverage than a new TPS would.

Cart haul from gerobak-only TPS. Separately, for the 151 TPS with no truck road within 100 m, cart_haul_to_truck_m is the routed distance a cart must cover to reach the truck network: median 184 m, p90 367 m, max 978 m; 29 exceed 300 m, 6 exceed 500 m. Worst: TPS RW 05 (Makasar, 978 m), TPS RW 002 Tegal Alur (Cengkareng, 824 m), Rw.005 pos giro (Duren Sawit, 569 m).

Per-TPS catchment load. Each TPS carries catchment_pop, catchment_gen_lday, capacity_vs_catchment. 80% (1,632 of 2,032) face more catchment demand than their effective capacity, median ratio 0.08 — consistent with much of the stream bypassing TPS via direct truck rounds and TPST rather than TPS being 12× undersized.

Outputs. service_coverage_20m.tif: 4 bands — collection haul (m), catchment ratio, serving mode, service class. Parcels carry collect_haul_m, serving_mode, catchment_ratio, service_class, detour_ratio.

Remaining limitations. Radii are planning parameters, not observed rounds — real catchments depend on crew numbers, ritasi (SNI allows 1–4/day), topography and local agreements. The network is unweighted by width or surface. Where FASIL is blank (960 TPS) or names only a container (117), the feed mode is unknown and the 500 m default applies — 1,077 of 2,036 TPS, or 53%. That is the single largest source of uncertainty here.

10 · Limitations

  1. KAPASITAS assumed liters, per-pickup throughput.
  2. Blank REMARK (80% of TPS) → daily pickup assumed, flagged.
  3. Within-kecamatan spread from WorldPop, which is itself modelled — the 100 m shape carries model uncertainty even after rescaling. BPS totals unaffected.
  4. Population is 2019 (BPS) / 2021 (WorldPop); TPS and land use are later vintages.
  5. Collection radii are planning parameters, not observed rounds; coverage % is assumption-sensitive (see Section 6), rankings robust. Network unweighted by width or surface.
  6. Road layer misses internal/compound roads (quantified: 17 FASIL conflicts).
  7. 53% of TPS have no usable feed-mode record (960 blank FASIL + 117 container-only), so collection-mode assignment is partial; those get a 500 m default radius.
  8. Non-residential generation remains area-based per land-use class.
  9. Waste also moves directly to trucks/TPST outside recorded TPS capacity.
